## Objective To assess customer churn over the past 12 months, identify its key drivers, and evaluate the financial and operational impact in order to inform retention-focused business decisions.

### Key Findings
	- Customers on month-to-month contracts demonstrate significantly higher churn rates than those on longer-term agreements.
	•	Churn is most pronounced during the early stages of the customer lifecycle, particularly within the first 12 months of tenure.
	•	Customers who churn represent a substantial loss in potential lifetime revenue, indicating a direct impact on long-term profitability.
	•	Certain payment methods are correlated with higher churn, pointing to potential friction in billing processes or customer experience.
	•	Inconsistent onboarding and limited proactive engagement reduce opportunities to build early customer loyalty.
  
### Risks
	•	Continued reliance on short-term contracts increases revenue volatility and customer attrition.
	•	High early-stage churn undermines customer acquisition investments.
	•	Billing or payment-related issues may negatively affect brand perception and trust.
	•	Absence of structured retention strategies limits the organisation’s ability to intervene before churn occurs.
### Recommendations
	•	Implement targeted early-tenure retention programs, including enhanced onboarding and proactive customer outreach.
	•	Incentivise migration from month-to-month to longer-term contracts through pricing or value-added benefits.
	•	Review and optimise payment processes to reduce friction and improve customer confidence.
	•	Establish a data-driven churn monitoring framework to enable timely, proactive retention actions.
